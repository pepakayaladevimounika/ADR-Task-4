# ADR-Task-4
# Task: Causality Assessment Report

## 1. Case Study Evaluation
* **Patient Demographics:** 68-year-old Male
* **Suspected Drug:** Lisinopril 10mg orally once daily (Initiated for Stage 1 Hypertension)
* **Adverse Event Reported:** Dry, hacking, non-productive cough.
* **Relevant History:** No history of asthma, seasonal allergies, or recent respiratory tract infections. Concurrently taking Metformin for Type 2 Diabetes.

## 2. Chronological Timeline

| Date | Event / Clinical Status |
| :--- | :--- |
| **October 01, 2026** | Patient begins Lisinopril 10mg once daily. Lung sounds clear. |
| **October 15, 2026** | Patient develops a persistent, dry, tickling cough. No fever or congestion. |
| **October 24, 2026** | **Dechallenge:** Lisinopril is stopped by the physician. |
| **October 29, 2026** | Cough resolves completely within 5 days of drug withdrawal. |

---

## 3. Causality Classification & Justification

### Official WHO-UMC Classification: PROBABLE / LIKELY

### Criterion-by-Criterion Justification:
1. **Temporal Relationship:** The dry cough began exactly 14 days after starting Lisinopril. This latency period is highly typical for ACE-inhibitor-induced coughs, which usually manifest within weeks to months of starting treatment.
2. **Dechallenge Evaluation:** **Positive Dechallenge.** The complete resolution of the cough within days of stopping Lisinopril confirms a drug-dependent response.
3. **Exclusion of Alternatives:** The patient has no history of chronic respiratory conditions (like COPD or asthma) and no signs of acute viral illness (no fever, rhinorrhea, or sore throat). The concurrent medication, Metformin, is not medically associated with respiratory adverse events.
4. **Rechallenge Status:** **Not Performed.** Reintroducing the drug was deemed unnecessary as the clinical link was strong, and alternative antihypertensive medications (like ARBs) were readily available to manage the patient's blood pressure.

---

## 4. Pharmacological Mechanism
Lisinopril is an Angiotensin-Converting Enzyme (ACE) inhibitor. By inhibiting the ACE enzyme, it prevents the degradation of **bradykinin** and **substance P** in the respiratory tract. The local accumulation of these inflammatory peptides sensitizes respiratory vagal afferent nerve fibers, triggering a dry, non-productive cough reflex in susceptible individuals.

## 5. Clinical Recommendation
* **Alternative Therapy:** Switch the patient from Lisinopril to an Angiotensin II Receptor Blocker (ARB) such as Losartan or Valsartan, which effectively controls blood pressure without altering bradykinin degradation pathway profiles.
